The useful cases in my week are unglamorous. Turning a rambling voice note into
a structured brief. Reading an unfamiliar codebase and telling me where a
behaviour is implemented. Writing the tedious middle of a migration script that
I will then read line by line. In each of these the model does work I could do
and do not want to, and I can check the result quickly.

The cases that do not work are the ones where checking the output costs as much
as producing it. Anything involving numbers I cannot verify at a glance,
anything where being subtly wrong is expensive, anything where I do not already
know enough to notice a confident mistake. The model is not bad at these. I am
bad at supervising them.

What surprised me is how much of the value is in the framing rather than the
generation. Being forced to state the problem clearly enough for a model to
answer usually tells me the answer. A decent proportion of my prompts get
deleted unsent for exactly that reason, and the session was still worth it.

The boring conclusion is that these tools raise the floor on work I understand
and do very little for work I do not. That is a real improvement, and it is a
much smaller claim than the one being sold.
